## PHASE 2: STRICT JOURNAL STRUCTURE

Generate one complete reflective journal for EACH module.

Every module must contain exactly FIVE sections, using these headings in this order:

### 1. Experience (Class Content)

**Exactly 80 words.**

Explain what the module covers from the student's perspective.

Requirements:
- Introduce the important topics and concepts.
- Explain the scope of the module.
- Mention the most significant principles or technical ideas.
- Connect related topics naturally.
- Show awareness of what the module aims to teach.
- Maintain first-person narration.
- Avoid inventing lectures, assignments, laboratory work, or classroom experiences.

Use natural expressions such as "I explored," "I understood," or "This module introduced me to," without repeating the same opening across modules.

### 2. Feelings (Emotional Reactions)

**Exactly 80 words.**

Describe genuine intellectual and emotional reactions to the module.

Requirements:
- Express curiosity, interest, uncertainty, or confidence where appropriate.
- Reflect on concepts that may be challenging.
- Explain why particular ideas are interesting or important.
- Show thoughtful reactions instead of generic praise.
- Demonstrate a developing attitude toward learning.
- Avoid exaggerated emotional statements.
- Do not claim personal experiences that were not provided.

Make the feelings specific to the actual module topics.

### 3. Learning (Key Analysis)

**Exactly 80 words.**

Explain the most important knowledge and conceptual understanding gained from the module.

Requirements:
- Identify important principles and key takeaways.
- Explain technical concepts using simple English.
- Demonstrate understanding rather than memorization.
- Show meaningful relationships between the module's topics.
- Explain why the concepts matter.
- Include thoughtful analysis or an important realization.
- Maintain technical correctness without sounding like a textbook.

This section should demonstrate the strongest evidence of academic understanding.

### 4. Application (Practical Use)

**Exactly 80 words.**

Explain how the knowledge can be used in practical, academic, professional, or real-world situations.

Requirements:
- Identify realistic applications directly related to the module.
- Explain how the concepts can help solve problems.
- Connect learning with future academic development.
- Discuss relevant technical or professional uses.
- Describe how I could apply the knowledge in future projects or situations.
- Avoid imaginary claims of completed activities.
- Keep applications realistic and specific.

Use future-oriented statements when actual practical experience has not been provided.

### 5. Conclusion

**Exactly 80 words.**

Provide a meaningful closing reflection for the module.

Requirements:
- Summarize the most valuable learning outcomes.
- Explain how the module contributes to personal or academic development.
- Reflect on any remaining areas for improvement.
- Identify realistic future learning goals.
- Show a sense of intellectual progress.
- End with a clear, meaningful takeaway.
- Avoid repeating complete sentences or ideas from earlier sections.

Each module must have its own distinct conclusion.

## PHASE 3: ADVANCED HUMAN-CENTERED WRITING RULES

Follow these writing principles throughout the entire output.

### A. First-Person Reflective Voice

Write consistently from the student's perspective.

Use natural first-person expressions such as:

- I learned that...
- I began to understand...
- I found it interesting that...
- I realized how...
- I was curious about...
- I found this concept challenging because...
- I can apply this knowledge...
- I want to improve my understanding of...

These are examples, not mandatory sentence starters.

Vary expressions across sections and modules.

### B. Simple and Natural English

Use clear, everyday academic English appropriate for a college student.

Avoid:
- Unnecessarily advanced vocabulary
- Overly formal expressions
- Robotic sentence structures
- Long and confusing explanations
- Excessive technical jargon
- Repeated sentence openings
- Generic motivational statements
- Unnecessary filler

Prefer short and medium-length sentences with smooth transitions.

### C. Genuine Reflection

Each section must demonstrate meaningful reflection rather than simply summarizing the syllabus.

Where appropriate, develop a natural progression from:

**Concept → Personal reaction → Understanding → Practical relevance → Future improvement**

Ensure that the writing reflects the actual subject matter.

### D. Originality and Variation

Every module must sound distinct.

Avoid repeating:
- Identical opening sentences
- The same emotional reactions
- The same practical applications
- Generic conclusions
- Similar paragraph structures across every module
- Repeated claims about confidence, knowledge, or interest

Adapt the language to the technical depth and nature of each module.

### E. Academic Integrity

Do not fabricate:
- Classroom discussions
- Teacher explanations
- Laboratory experiments
- Projects or assignments
- Examination results
- Personal achievements
- Events or activities

If actual experiences are not provided, reflect on the syllabus content and possible learning experiences without presenting imagined events as facts.

Do not make claims about AI detection or guaranteed evaluation scores.

Revise weak sections before presenting the final output.
